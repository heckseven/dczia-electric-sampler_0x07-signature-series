## The main loop is ~7 ms per pass, not 200 us

**measured.** `min=5279 p50=7049 p99=15655 max=33416`, microseconds, over 4000 passes
with the transport playing a four-track pattern.

This settles a contradiction the repo has carried for some time:

| source | claim | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `sequencer.py:237` | "a main loop that is otherwise around 200 us" | **wrong by ~35x** |
| `handoff-2026-08-28.md:171` | ~125 passes/s while streaming, i.e. 8 ms | consistent |
| `streaming-bug-rootcause.md:706` | 3000 passes in 20.32 s, i.e. 6.8 ms | consistent |

The bucket counts say it is not a long tail hiding a fast typical case: 3,439 of 4,000
passes fall in 5-10 ms and 552 in 10-20 ms. Seven milliseconds is what the loop *is*.

Two consequences for the rewrite:

- **A pad can be serviced at most once per ~7 ms**, whatever the keypad scan interval
  is. The 20 ms scan is not the binding constraint; it is one term alongside a larger
  one.
- **The worst pass, 33.4 ms, is longer than the entire 32 ms audio buffer.** Whatever
  causes it, the audio path cannot be waiting on this loop.

### Caveat, and it matters

This was measured with **no state machine attached** - the loop body was `guard.feed()`,
the GC floor check and `engine.tick()`, with `machine.update()` absent. The real
`main.py` also renders the display and the LEDs. So **7 ms is a lower bound** on the
shipping loop, and the next run must attach `SamplerState`.

## time.monotonic_ns() resolves to ~30.5 us, not nanoseconds

**measured.** `min_step_ns = median_step_ns = 30518`, which is exactly 1/32768 s.

Fine for millisecond-scale work and quietly wrong for anything finer, which is why this
case runs first. Every figure in this document is quoted at that granularity. The
occasional `max_step_ns=976563` (1/1024 s) is a tick boundary.

`backwards=0` over 2000 samples: monotonic, as advertised.

## A collection is 14-16 ms, not 25-27

**measured.** `min=14465 p50=15258 max=16204`, over 24 collections with real garbage
generated between each.

`handoff-2026-08-28.md:169` records 25-27 ms. Both are probably right for the heap they
were measured on - that one was taken when the sample budget was 48 KB and free memory
sat near the floor; this one has `free_after=73440`. **A collection costs roughly half
the audio buffer**, and the number moves with how full the heap is, so the rewrite
should treat it as a function of occupancy rather than a constant.

`free_floor=16288` during the loop run, against `GC_FLOOR = 16384` in `main.py` - the
loop was collecting at the floor, six times in 4000 passes.

## The keypad constants cannot be read from the object

**not measured.** `interval=unreadable debounce_threshold=unreadable`.
`keypad.KeyMatrix` exposes neither, so the 20 ms scan interval recorded in
`streaming-bug-rootcause.md` section 16 remains **inferred from upstream CircuitPython
source**, not confirmed on this chip - and the badge runs a DCZia-supplied `.uf2` whose
provenance is unrecorded.

Closing it needs a timing experiment rather than a property read: drive a key event and
measure the distribution of detection delays, which should be uniform over one scan
interval. Not yet done.

## Confirmed from the live objects

**measured.** `key_count=12`, `mixer_voices=18`, `mixer_rate=16000`, `ram_budget=32768`,
`ram_used=32768` - the kit exactly fills its budget - and `midi_poll_ms=2`.

Note `MIDI_POLL_MS = 2` is the *serial* MIDI poll. The USB MIDI poll is a separate
constant and is the 20 ms one that matters for the planned MIDI-stimulus measurement.
