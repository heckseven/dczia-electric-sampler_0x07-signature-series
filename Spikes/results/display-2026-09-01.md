# Task 7: display cost and the supply-coupling artefact - 2026-09-01

## The null test: the pop is not digital

`sequencer.py` stops the I2S stream whenever nothing is sounding, and explains why:

> *"An active stream makes the amplifier sensitive to traffic on the shared supply, and
> the display is on that supply: with the stream running, redrawing the screen pops
> audibly; with it stopped, the identical redrawing is silent. Confirmed on the badge."*

Confirmed by ear establishes that something pops, not where. So: run a deterministic
waveform through the real I2S path, checksum every output word over 400 blocks with the
display bus idle, then over 400 blocks with it hammering full frames continuously, and
compare.

**measured:**

```
blocks=400 quiet_sum=0x0aba1900 busy_sum=0x0aba1900 identical=1
quiet_gap_us=2000 busy_gap_us=2000 block_period_us=2000
```

**Bit-identical.** Not close - the same 32-bit order-sensitive checksum over 12,800
samples, with the display doing nothing and with it writing a 512-byte frame back to
back. The refill interval never moved off the 2,000 us block period in either phase.

So the digital path is untouched, and **the artefact is downstream of everything software
can reach.** There is no timing fix, no priority fix, no buffering fix. That is a
genuinely useful negative: it closes off the entire class of software remedies that would
otherwise have been worth trying first.

## What a frame actually costs: 12.8 ms of bus, 38 us of CPU

**measured**, DMA'd over the RP2040's I2C, 20 trials each, CPU time separated from bus
time by timestamping between starting the DMA and waiting on it:

| bus | payload | total | **CPU** | CPU share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 399 kHz | 512 B (full frame) | 12,783 us | **38 us** | 0.30% |
| 399 kHz | 128 B (one page) | 3,234 us | **10 us** | 0.31% |
| 1.00 MHz | 512 B (full frame) | 5,579 us | **37 us** | 0.66% |
| 1.00 MHz | 128 B (one page) | 1,405 us | **10 us** | 0.71% |

Two things fall out.

**The 32 ms in the project notes is mostly software, not bus.** The bus floor for 513
bytes at 400 kHz is about 11.6 ms and the measured transfer is 12.78 ms - 91% efficient.
`displayio` spends the other ~19 ms somewhere that is not the wire. A DMA'd C driver gets
the wall time down to the bus floor and the **CPU cost down to 38 microseconds**, which is
0.3% of one core.

**1 MHz is 2.29x faster and worked cleanly.** `setup.py` records that 1 MHz was tried,
measured "a useful 5 ms faster on the worst stall", looked clean, and was backed out
because it was running during a run of unrelated instability. Measured here: 5,579 us
against 12,783, a 7.2 ms saving per frame, over 40 consistent transfers at both sizes with
no retries or stalls. That is not a soak test - it is 40 transfers, not 40 minutes - but
the reason it was abandoned no longer applies, and the measurement supports revisiting it.

## The load-step budget - derived, not measured

The artefact itself cannot be measured by instrumented firmware. There is no on-badge
sensor, and the RP2040's ADC reference derives from the same 3V3 rail, so it cancels
exactly the quantity of interest.

What the datasheets give:

| part | figure | source |
|---|---|---|
| MAX98357A PSRR | **75 dB typ at 217 Hz**, **60 dB typ at 10 kHz** (200 mV p-p ripple) | MAX98357A/B, Electrical Characteristics |
| MAX98357A output | 0.93 W into 8 ohm at VDD 3.7 V, THD+N 10% | same |
| MAX98357A turn-on | 7 ms typ, 7.5 ms max | same, via Task 5 |
| RT6150B (Pico W 3V3) | buck-boost, 800 mA, switching 800 kHz - 1.2 MHz | Richtek RT6150A/B |
| Pico W 3V3 guidance | keep external load under 300 mA | Raspberry Pi Pico datasheet |

**The amp is the victim, not the aggressor, and its rejection is 15 dB worse at the
frequencies that matter.** PSRR falls from 75 dB at 217 Hz to 60 dB at 10 kHz. A load step
is a broadband event whose energy is concentrated in its edges, so it lands where the amp
rejects least. That is consistent with the symptom being a *pop* rather than a hum.

**The aggressor is most plausibly the OLED panel**, and the argument is one of
correlation rather than magnitude: the NeoPixels draw a roughly constant PWM load that is
not synchronised with anything, and the amp's own draw is the signal. The only load that
steps *when a redraw happens* is the panel, whose charge pump current follows how many
pixels are lit - and the symptom is specifically that redrawing pops.

Stated as the weakest link in this section: **no current was measured for any of the
three.** This names the dominant contributor by reasoning about which load is correlated
with the event, not by measuring amperes, and a scope on the 3V3 rail would settle in one
minute what this paragraph argues at length.

## Disposition: software can shorten the pop, not remove it, and the current remedy is too expensive

The null test rules out a software *cause*. What software still controls is the
**duration of the disturbance**, and that is now a measured quantity:

| strategy | disturbance window | vs today |
|---|---|---|
| full frame at 400 kHz (today) | 12.78 ms | - |
| full frame at 1 MHz | 5.58 ms | 2.3x shorter |
| one page at 400 kHz | 3.23 ms | 4.0x shorter |
| **one page at 1 MHz** | **1.41 ms** | **9.1x shorter** |

And the current remedy has a price Task 5 put a number on. Stopping the stream when idle
means the amp drops into standby, and coming back costs `tON` - **7 ms typical, 7.5 ms
max**. Against a 2.56 ms trigger-to-output latency with the stream running, that is
roughly **10 ms for the first hit after any pause: the entire budget, spent on the note a
player is most likely to notice.**

**Recommendation: stop stopping the stream.** Keep BCLK running, stream silence when
idle, and cut the disturbance instead - move the panel to 1 MHz and redraw in partial
windows. That trades an artefact that has been made 9x shorter for 7 ms off the worst-case
latency of every first hit.

**The deferred item is closed: no pop.** (2026-09-02.)

The Phase 1 firmware does exactly what this section recommended - stream always running,
partial-window redraws, and an idle screen that writes nothing at all - and the player
reported no pops while playing with the display updating live.

That settles the trade. **Keep the stream running.** The 7 ms amp wake-up is avoided, the
worst-case trigger-to-output stays at 4.56 ms instead of roughly 10 ms, and the artefact
that justified stopping the stream is not audible once the disturbance is short enough.

Reported by ear, which is the appropriate instrument: the question was always whether a
person can hear it, and the null test had already established there is nothing digital
left to measure. What is *not* established is a bound - one session, one badge, one
player, one set of samples. A pop that appears only on a particular redraw pattern would
not have shown up. The fallback costs 7 ms and still works, so the risk of being wrong
here is bounded and reversible.

## Caveats

- 20 trials per configuration, all writes, no reads back from the panel. Consistent to
  within 11 us at every size, so the mean is solid, but this is not a soak.
- The 1 MHz result is 40 transfers, not 40 minutes. SSD1306's datasheet maximum is
  400 kHz; 1 MHz is out of spec and worked, which is evidence and not a guarantee.
- The null test proves the display does not perturb the digital path *on this workload*.
  A design that later shares a DMA channel or an IRQ priority between the panel and the
  audio path would need it re-run.
- No current was measured. The load-step section is datasheet arithmetic and a
  correlation argument, and it is labelled as such throughout.
