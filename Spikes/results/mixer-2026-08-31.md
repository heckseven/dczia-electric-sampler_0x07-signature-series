# Task 4: mixer cost and bank contention - 2026-08-31

What a fixed-point mixer costs on this chip, measured with SysTick over 200 blocks of
32 frames, at 125 MHz.

## The mixer is not the constraint, and it was never close

**measured**, uncontended, samples in the striped SRAM:

| voices | plain, cyc/block | % of core | linear, cyc/block | % of core |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 709 | 0.2 | 1,207 | 0.4 |
| 4 | 2,196 | 0.8 | 4,172 | 1.6 |
| 8 | 4,185 | 1.6 | 8,129 | 3.2 |
| **18** | **9,173** | **3.7** | **18,026** | **7.2** |
| 24 | 12,169 | 4.8 | 23,972 | 9.5 |

A 32-frame block at 16 kHz is 2.00 ms, which is 250,000 cycles. The firmware's 18 voices
- 8 tracks doubled, plus audition and metronome - cost **7.2% of one core** with linear
interpolation and 3.7% without.

Per voice-frame that is **31.3 cycles** interpolated, 15.9 flat. Extrapolated to the
whole core, the ceiling is about **250 voices** interpolated, 490 flat.

## The planning estimate was 3x optimistic and still reached the right conclusion

The spec guessed ~10 cycles per voice-frame, hence ~2% of a core, and concluded that
throughput was not the problem - determinism was. The guess assumed an instruction set
this chip does not have: Cortex-M0+ has no `SMULL`, no saturating arithmetic, no free
operand shifts, and only `r0`-`r7` for most instructions.

Measured, it is 31.3 cycles - **3.1x the guess**. And 7.2% instead of 2.3%.

The conclusion survives the correction with room to spare, which is the useful part. At
18 voices the mixer has **13x headroom** on a dedicated core. Nothing about the voice
count, the interpolation, or the arithmetic width is a design constraint. Every real
problem in `streaming-bug-rootcause.md` was a stall or an allocation, and this confirms
there is no throughput problem hiding underneath them.

## Interpolation costs 97%, not 5%

**measured**: 15.9 cycles per voice-frame flat, 31.3 interpolated - the second load, the
subtract, the second multiply and the shift roughly double the inner loop.

The spec's "about 5% more with interpolation" was wrong by a factor of twenty. It does
not matter at these absolute numbers, but it is the kind of error that would matter if
it were repeated somewhere with less headroom.

## Contention costs under 5%, and bank placement barely matters

**measured**, 18 voices with linear interpolation, against a DMA writing flat out into
the striped SRAM with no DREQ throttle. `churn_busy=1` on both contended rows, so the
churn was still running when the window closed:

| samples in | quiet | contended | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| striped SRAM | 18,031 | 18,870 | **+4.7%** |
| `SCRATCH_Y` | 18,032 | 18,781 | **+4.2%** |

Worst case is 7.5% of one core instead of 7.2%. The headroom argument is unchanged.

Two things follow. First, the contention term is real but small, and it is an upper
bound by construction: an unthrottled DMA moves a word roughly every cycle, where the
real I2S stream at 16 kHz mono moves one every 7,812. Second, **moving sample data to a
scratch bank buys almost nothing** - 0.5 percentage points. That is the expected result
once stated plainly: only the sample reads moved, while the mixer's code, its stack and
its output buffer are all still in striped SRAM, so most of the contended traffic stayed
where it was. Scratch-bank placement is not a tool worth spending the rewrite's
complexity budget on.

## Two earlier attempts that faked a null result

Before the numbers above, two designs produced confident wrong answers rather than
errors. Worth recording, because both failure modes are easy to repeat:

1. **A single DMA channel chained to itself.** On RP2040, `CHAIN_TO` naming the
   channel's own number *disables* chaining. The churn ran 1,024 words - roughly a
   thousand cycles against a three-million-cycle window - and the result read
   `contended=1 ... 18,018` against `contended=0 ... 18,026`. Contention looked free. It
   had simply not happened.

2. **A pair of channels chained to each other.** That does run continuously, but it
   cannot be stopped the obvious way: aborting a channel that another channel chains to
   lets the partner restart it, so `dma_hw->abort` never clears. The badge wedged in
   that spin with USB unserviced.

Both were replaced by one unchained channel with a 2^28 transfer count and a 4 KB write
ring - continuous by construction, and stoppable because nothing restarts it. The run
now also reports `churn_busy`, which is what turns "contention was free" into a claim
that can be checked rather than assumed. It reads 1 on both contended rows above.

## The recovery contract had a hole, found by falling into it

`spike_begin` held still for `SPIKE_CDC_WINDOW_MS` with `sleep_ms`. It **held still but
did not listen** - so the window that exists to guarantee the host a way back in could
not accept `B`, the one command that gets back to BOOTSEL.

The consequence is exactly the failure the contract was written to prevent: a spike that
wedges before reaching its pump loop is unreachable by software, and the badge has to be
recovered by holding BOOTSEL while replugging. `spike_begin` now polls for `B` through
the hold instead of sleeping through it.

### The watchdog was paused by a pin nobody is driving - confirmed by fixing it

`spike_begin` armed it as `watchdog_enable(SPIKE_WATCHDOG_MS, true)`. That second
argument is `pause_on_debug`, and in the SDK it sets three bits:

```c
uint32_t dbg_bits = WATCHDOG_CTRL_PAUSE_DBG0_BITS |
                    WATCHDOG_CTRL_PAUSE_DBG1_BITS |
                    WATCHDOG_CTRL_PAUSE_JTAG_BITS;
```

Any of them pauses the counter while the debug interface looks active, and this badge
exposes SWD pads with nothing driving them. With `true`, the wedged image sat
enumerated-but-dead for **over an hour** with an 8 s watchdog armed and never fed.

**measured**, after rearming with `pause_on_debug = false`: the badge resets on schedule.
The port drops and the USB device number steps - 062 to 063 - on every cycle. Same
silicon, same spin, one bit different.

Nothing in this campaign is debugged over SWD, and an unattended run needs a watchdog
that cannot be talked out of firing, so the pause stays off.

## What the recovery contract actually looks like now

All three legs are exercised, and the last two were only exercised because something
genuinely broke:

| leg | state |
|---|---|
| heartbeat resets a silent-host badge | **measured** - and is why the spike reboots every 30 s after `DONE`, which is the design working, not a fault |
| watchdog resets a wedged badge | **measured**, once `pause_on_debug` was off |
| boot window accepts `B` | **measured** - the running image answered and went to BOOTSEL |

Both holes were in the part of the design meant to make failure survivable, and a single
wedge went through both: a window that held still without listening, and a watchdog a
floating pin could pause. Neither would have been found by reasoning about the design.
Both showed up the first time something actually hung, which is the argument for Phase 0
existing at all.

The one step still needing hands is the first BOOTSEL after a hard wedge. With the
window listening and the watchdog firing, a spike now has to defeat both to get there.
