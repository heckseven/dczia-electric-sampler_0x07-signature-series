# Task 5: output latency and the flash hazard - 2026-09-01

The two numbers that decide the rewrite's audio architecture. Both measured on the badge
through its own amp wiring - GP0 BCLK, GP1 LRCLK, GP2 DATA, the pins `sequencer.py`
already asks `audiobusio.I2SOut` for.

## (a) Trigger to output: 2.56 ms worst case, against a 10 ms budget

**measured**, 300 dithered trials per block size, timestamped at the pin by a second PIO
state machine rather than at the DMA transfer count:

| block | period | min | mean | **worst** | fixed term |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 frames | 1.00 ms | 1.57 ms | 2.07 ms | **2.56 ms** | 566 us |
| 32 frames | 2.00 ms | 2.57 ms | 3.57 ms | **4.56 ms** | 566 us |
| 64 frames | 4.00 ms | 4.57 ms | 6.57 ms | **8.56 ms** | 573 us |
| 128 frames | 8.00 ms | 8.57 ms | 12.56 ms | **16.56 ms** | 573 us |

The law is clean:

```
latency = block_period x (1 .. 2)  +  568 us
```

Min lands one block period after the trigger, worst lands two, and the mean sits halfway
- which is what a double-buffered design has to pay, because a sample triggered just
after a refill waits for the buffer in flight plus its own.

**Three of the four sizes fit the budget.** 16 frames uses a quarter of it. 64 frames
uses 86%, which is real but leaves nothing for anything else. 128 frames does not fit.

### The 568 us is mostly one line of setup

The fixed term is not mysterious: the PIO TX FIFO is joined to 8 words deep, and 8
frames at 16 kHz is exactly **500 us**. The remaining ~68 us is PIO serialisation and
the detect path. Unjoining the FIFO halves that contribution to 250 us, at the cost of
half the underrun margin - a knob worth having, not worth turning yet.

The 13 us serialisation offset of the trigger value's first set bit is already subtracted
in the table; it is reported as `correction_us` in the raw records rather than folded in
silently.

### The distribution is real, not one number repeated

`min` to `max` spans exactly one block period at every size, which is what the dithered
trigger is there to prove. A fixed-phase self-trigger would have returned a single value
four times and looked like a much more precise result than it was.

`lost=0` at every size, and `max_refill_gap_us` equalled the block period exactly
throughout - the refill never once ran late during the latency runs.

## (b) The flash hazard is real in size and fully mitigable

**measured**: erasing and programming one 4 KB sector takes **~35 ms**, during which XIP
is gone. That is 35x the smallest block period and 4.4x the largest. If any of the audio
path lived in flash it would be a catastrophic dropout.

It does not, and the result is unambiguous:

| block | period | erase | max refill gap, quiet | max refill gap, **during erase** | underran |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 frames | 1.00 ms | 35.97 ms | 1.000 ms | **1.000 ms** | no |
| 32 frames | 2.00 ms | 35.91 ms | 2.000 ms | **2.000 ms** | no |
| 64 frames | 4.00 ms | 34.53 ms | 4.000 ms | **4.000 ms** | no |
| 128 frames | 8.00 ms | 35.77 ms | 8.000 ms | **8.000 ms** | no |

**Zero underruns, and not a microsecond of added jitter.** The refill interval during a
35 ms erase is indistinguishable from the refill interval with nothing happening, and the
refill count confirms the stream never paused - 36 refills across 36 ms at a 1 ms period.

What makes it work is exactly three things, all of which the rewrite has to keep:

1. The refill ISR and the fill routine are `__not_in_flash_func`.
2. The ISR calls nothing that lives in flash - the timestamp is a bare `timer_hw->timerawl`
   register read, not `time_us_32()`.
3. Every interrupt except the audio DMA is masked for the operation. USB above all: its
   handler is in flash and would fetch garbage the moment XIP went away.

**The cost is stated honestly.** For those 35 ms the UI is frozen and USB is deaf -
keypad, encoders, display and MIDI all stop. For saving a song or a setting that is
invisible. For anything that wants to happen *during* a performance it is not, and the
rewrite should treat a flash write as an operation with a 35 ms UI stall, scheduled
rather than incidental.

The erase target is the top sector, `0x101FF000`, declared as a constant and checked at
runtime against `__flash_binary_end` (`0x10008A34`) before anything is erased. The check
is in the raw record. An erase landing in the loaded image would brick the only badge
into a state whose one recovery is holding BOOTSEL while replugging.

## (c) The amp: derived from the datasheet, not measured

From the MAX98357A/MAX98357B datasheet, Electrical Characteristics:

| parameter | symbol | conditions | value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turn-On Time | tON | - | **7 ms typ, 7.5 ms max** |
| Click-and-Pop Level | KCP | peak, +25C, A-weighted, 32 samples/s | **-72 dBV** into shutdown, **-66 dBV** out of shutdown |
| Standby Current | ISTNDBY | SD_MODE = 1.8V, no BCLK | 340 uA typ |
| Quiescent Current | IDD | VDD = 3.7V | 2.4 mA typ |

**Group delay is not specified.** Searched the whole document: the string does not appear,
and no digital-filter latency figure is given. That is an absence, not a small number -
anything claiming a value for it would be inventing one.

Two behaviours matter more than the numbers:

> *"The ICs automatically enter standby mode when BCLK is removed."*

> *"The MAX98357 does not have a volume ramp-down response when entering shutdown. For
> optimal click-and-pop performance, ramp down the digital data on SDIN before powering
> down."*

### Verdict on stop-the-stream-when-idle: don't

The plan wanted this priced. It is priced at **7 ms**, and that settles it. Stopping BCLK
drops the amp into standby, and the next hit pays `tON` *before* the block latency -
turning a 2.56 ms worst case into roughly 10 ms and consuming the entire budget to save
2 mA. Keep BCLK running continuously and stream silence when idle.

That also disposes of the pop for free. The datasheet's own advice is to ramp SDIN down
before shutdown; a design that never enters shutdown never takes the -66 dBV hit on the
way out. **Software-mitigable, and mitigated by a decision the latency budget forces
anyway.**

## What this means for the rewrite

Task 5 was the go/no-go, on the grounds that the flash hazard would constrain a C rewrite
exactly as much as it constrains the current firmware. **It does not.** Pinning the audio
path to SRAM removes it completely, and that option does not exist in CircuitPython.

Recommended block size: **32 frames**. Worst case 4.56 ms is half the budget, leaving
room for the mixer's 7.5% and for the FIFO to stay joined. 16 frames is available if
something later needs 2.56 ms, at twice the interrupt rate.

## Caveats

- **300 trials per block size, not the 10,000 the plan asked for.** At 128 frames a trial
  costs about 48 ms, and 10,000 would run 8 minutes per size. The distributions are flat
  and span exactly one block period, so more trials would sharpen the mean and not move
  the worst case. Stated rather than quietly applied.
- The refill here writes a constant, not a mixed block. Mixing cost is Task 4's number
  and adds 7.5% of a core to the ISR; it does not change the latency law, which is set by
  buffering rather than by the work done inside a buffer.
- One block size, one erase per size. The 35 ms figure is consistent across four runs but
  is not a distribution, and flash erase time is temperature- and wear-dependent.
- `underran` is derived from the refill interval, not from listening. A gap never exceeded
  the block period, so nothing replayed a stale buffer - but this is a timing argument,
  not an acoustic one.
