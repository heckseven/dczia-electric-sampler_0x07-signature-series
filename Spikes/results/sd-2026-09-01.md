# Task 6: storage timing - 2026-09-01

Measured through a raw block driver over SPI with DMA, no filesystem. FatFS reentrancy
is what broke streaming in the Python, so putting FatFS back in would measure the layer
that failed rather than the card underneath it.

One card: the 32 GB (as CircuitPython reads it) card that lives in the badge.

## Per-block reads: 478 us typical, 1.3 ms worst over 60 seconds

**measured**, sequential single-block reads wrapping inside a 64 MB region, 60 s per
clock:

| requested | actual | reads | fail | min | p50 | p99 | p99.9 | **max** | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 MHz | **20.83 MHz** | 125,207 | 0 | 320 us | 450 us | 760 us | 770 us | **1,318 us** | 1,046 KB/s |
| 16 MHz | 15.63 MHz | 110,100 | 0 | 399 us | 530 us | 820 us | 850 us | 928 us | 919 KB/s |
| 8 MHz | 7.81 MHz | 72,283 | 0 | 717 us | 840 us | 860 us | 1,130 us | 1,157 us | 603 KB/s |

A second, independent 60 s pass at each clock agreed closely - 476 vs 478 us mean at the
top clock, 543 vs 544, 829 vs 829 - with an 848 us maximum where the first run saw 1,318:

| clock | run 1 mean / max | run 2 mean / max |
|---|---|---|
| 20.83 MHz | 478 / 1,318 us | 476 / 848 us |
| 15.63 MHz | 544 / 928 us | 543 / 930 us |
| 7.81 MHz | 829 / 1,157 us | 829 / 1,157 us |

**615,698 reads across both runs, zero failures.** About six minutes of continuous
reading with no error and no stall longer than 1.4 ms.

The requested clocks are not the clocks you get. `clk_peri` is 125 MHz and the SPI
prescaler only reaches it in steps, so 24 MHz becomes 125/6 = 20.83 MHz and 8 MHz becomes
125/16 = 7.81 MHz. **31.25 MHz (125/4) was not swept** - it is the next step up and might
go faster still, which is a gap in this run rather than a limit of the hardware.

## Loading a kit: 1,524 KB/s

**measured** at 20.83 MHz, sequential:

| size | time | rate |
|---|---|---|
| 32 KB | 21.0 ms | 1,523 KB/s |
| 64 KB | 42.0 ms | 1,524 KB/s |
| 128 KB | 84.0 ms | 1,524 KB/s |

Perfectly linear, so filling the whole 246.8 KB free pool from the card would take about
**162 ms**. Kit loading is a boot-time cost and it is not a problem.

Note the gap between 1,524 KB/s here and the 1,046 KB/s mean during the soak: the soak
pays full command overhead per block through `CMD17`, while the kit loop reads
back-to-back. Neither uses `CMD18` multi-block, which would close more of it.

## Streaming is feasible, and the insurance is affordable

This is the question behind "can a sample be longer than RAM". Working from the measured
mean of 478 us per block, at 16 kHz mono 16-bit (32,000 B/s, 62.5 blocks/s per voice):

| voices | blocks/s | share of the card's time |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 250 | 12.0% |
| 8 | 500 | 23.9% |
| 18 | 1,125 | 53.8% |

Eight streaming voices need about a quarter of the card. That is comfortable.

The real design question is not throughput but the tail, and it is answered with buffer:

| stall to survive | per voice | 8 voices | share of free SRAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.4 ms (worst seen) | 45 B | 358 B | 0% |
| 100 ms | 3.2 KB | 25.6 KB | 10% |
| **200 ms** | **6.4 KB** | **51.2 KB** | **20%** |
| 500 ms | 16 KB | 128 KB | 51% |

**200 ms of insurance for eight voices costs 51 KB** - a fifth of the free pool, leaving
195 KB. That is the number that makes streaming a real option rather than a hope.

### The honest limit of this result

**A clean 60 s run does not bound the tail.** The hazard streaming actually faces is a
card garbage-collection stall of hundreds of milliseconds, which can arrive far less
often than once a minute. Three minutes of reading with a 1.3 ms maximum says the typical
case is excellent and says **nothing** about whether a 300 ms stall happens once an hour.

The plan anticipated exactly this and set the trigger accordingly: run the extended
campaign **if and only if a later phase actually selects streaming**. That trigger has
not been pulled. What this run licenses is "streaming is worth designing for", not
"streaming is safe".

Also: one card, one filesystem state, one temperature. Cards differ enormously in their
stall behaviour, and a cheap card is where this would bite.

## Two workarounds the Python carries

**The discarded first read is not needed here.** `setup.py` deliberately spends one read
after init because some cards fail it. Read the same block twice after bring-up and both
succeeded with identical checksums (`first_read_ok=1 second_read_ok=1`, sums equal), so
this card does not need it. That is one card - the workaround costs one 512-byte read and
should stay until it has been disproved on more of them.

**The 22-bit `C_SIZE` handling is still needed, and getting it wrong silently halves the
card.** The raw CSD, read from the card:

```
400e00325b590001dd7f7f800a40000d
```

`CSD_STRUCTURE` is 1, so this is CSD v2, and `C_SIZE` is the 22 bits at 69:48 -
`csd[7] & 0x3F` for the top six, then `csd[8]` and `csd[9]`. `csd[7]` is `0x01`, so bit 16
is genuinely set:

| computed as | C_SIZE | blocks | capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **22 bits, per the spec** | 122,239 | 125,173,760 | **59.7 GiB / 64 GB** |
| 16 bits, dropping `csd[7]` | 56,703 | 58,064,896 | 27.7 GiB / 30 GB |

CircuitPython's `sdcardio` reported `count() = 58064896` on this same card - **an exact
match for the 16-bit computation**, to the block. The card is a 64 GB SDXC and one of the
two drivers is reading it as 30 GB.

Stated carefully: what is measured is the raw CSD and both drivers' answers. The exact
match with a 16-bit truncation is strong evidence of where the difference comes from, but
CircuitPython's source was not read, so "consistent with dropping the high six bits" is
as far as this goes.

It does no harm on *this* card, because the FAT32 partition ends at block 58,000,000,
just inside the truncated count. On a card partitioned beyond 29.7 GB it would silently
address the wrong blocks. **A C driver must do the full 22 bits.**

## Why the badge could not mount this card - and it was my doing

Not in the plan, and worth recording because the cause was a tool in this campaign.

The badge had been booting from flash samples with `setup.py` reporting "No SD Card
Found!", while the card itself read 615,000 blocks without a single error. Chased through
the REPL, the real exception was not an I/O failure at all:

```
storage.mount(storage.VfsFat(c), '/sd')
  -> RuntimeError: Mount point directory missing
```

**CircuitPython's `storage.mount()` needs `/sd` to exist as a real directory in the root
filesystem, and nothing in this project creates it** - not `Tools/build.py`, which only
copies, and not `setup.py`, which only mounts. It had simply always been there.

`Spikes/host/restore_sampler.py` deleted it. That script clears the volume before
deploying, because `build.py` never removes and stale modules otherwise shadow the build
- and its keep-list was `boot_out.txt` and `System Volume Information`. `/sd` went with
everything else, on the first restore and every one after. The card, the wiring, the
driver and the filesystem were all fine the whole time.

Recreating the directory fixed it immediately: `sd_baudrate=24000000`, `sd_error=None`,
and the card's `kits`, `samples`, `songs` and `settings.prefs` all present and readable.
`restore_sampler.py` now keeps `sd` and recreates it if it is missing.

Two things worth carrying into the rewrite:

- **A missing mount point is indistinguishable from a missing card to the player.** The
  screen says "No SD Card Found!" for a fault that has nothing to do with the card.
  `setup.py` catches `RuntimeError` alongside `OSError` precisely because the mount raises
  it, and then reports both as the same thing. Distinguishing them costs one branch.
- **The deploy is not complete until the directories the firmware needs exist.** A build
  step that only copies files cannot express "this directory must exist", and git does not
  track empty directories either, so nothing in the repository records the requirement.

### The one thing this run did not settle

Every freshly constructed `sdcardio.SDCard` fails its first `readblocks`, at every
baudrate tried, and succeeds on the next. That is exactly the behaviour `setup.py`'s
`wake()` exists to absorb, so it is handled - but the C driver saw no such failure, which
suggests it is `sdcardio`'s init sequence rather than the card. Not chased further,
because it costs one 512-byte read and hurts nothing.
